STEP 1

Create Your First Practice Event

On the Cue the Crowd screen to the right, choose Create New Event.

Fill it out just as you would if you were preparing for a real client. For your first trial, we recommend creating a wedding.

Example:
Event Name: Emily & Jake's Wedding
Event Type: Wedding
Date: Any future date
Guest count: Make one up

Complete the event setup questions and save the event. Cue the Crowd will create the event and generate its unique guest connection information.

STEP 2

Event HQ — Your Starting Point

DJ / HOST SIDE

After creating an event, you'll enter Event HQ. Think of Event HQ as the home base for that particular event.

From here you can prepare the entertainment before the event and control different parts of the experience while the event is happening.

You'll see tools such as Guest Hub, Show Builder, Host Control, Show Director, Audience Display and other event features.

STEP 3

Show Builder — Plan Before the Event

DJ / HOST SIDE

Show Builder is where you can prepare interactive entertainment ahead of time.

Instead of trying to invent something while you're standing behind the DJ booth, you can build a sequence of activities before the event.

Now let's run one feature all the way through so you can see exactly how the DJ side and the guest's phone communicate in real time.

Wedding example:

You might prepare a Bride's Side vs. Groom's Side competition, a music challenge, a prediction, a buzzer round and a crowd activity.

The activities are saved with the event so they're ready when you need them.

STEP 4

Host Control — Control What Happens Live

DJ / HOST SIDE

Host Control is your private control area during the event.

Guests do not see these controls. You decide what becomes available to them and when.

Depending on the activity, you can launch voting, predictions, buzzers, competitions, song requests, shoutouts and other interactive features.

Example:

Dinner is running 15 minutes late. Instead of simply waiting, you launch a quick Bride's Side vs. Groom's Side competition from Host Control.
STEP 5

Guest Hub — Now See What Your Guests See

GUEST PHONE SIDE

This is one of the most important parts of your trial.

Guest Hub is the page your guests access from their phones.

At a real event, guests scan the event's QR code once. There is no app for them to download and they do not need a Cue the Crowd account.

Find the Guest Hub link or QR code for your practice event.

Then either:

  • scan the QR code with your actual phone, or
  • open the guest link in another browser/private window.

Keep the DJ/Host screen open too.

You should now have:

Computer: You are the DJ controlling the event.

Phone or second window: You are pretending to be a guest.

This lets you experience exactly how Cue the Crowd connects the DJ and the audience.

STEP 6

Try a Live Buzzer From Both Sides

Let's run one feature all the way through.

FIRST — DJ SIDE

From the host controls, open or launch a Fastest Finger / Buzzer activity.

THEN — GUEST SIDE

Look at your phone or guest browser window. The guest should now have access to the buzzer.

Tap the buzzer as the guest.

BACK TO DJ SIDE

Return to Host Control. The DJ can see who buzzed first.

That's the basic Cue the Crowd flow:

DJ launches something → Guest participates from their phone → DJ receives the result.

Voting, predictions, competitions, song requests and many other features use this same connected-event concept.

STEP 7

Show Director — When You Need Entertainment Now

DJ / HOST SIDE

Show Director helps you run structured entertainment rather than simply launching isolated features.

It can guide you through rounds and activities while you're working the room.

Example:

The caterer tells you dinner needs another 10 minutes.

Instead of scrambling for something to say, you can pull up an interactive activity and turn those 10 minutes into entertainment.

Cue the Crowd includes different types of experiences for weddings, corporate events, parties, proms and other audiences.

STEP 8

Audience Display — The Big Screen View

Audience Display is different from both Host Control and Guest Hub.

Host Control stays private on the DJ's computer.

Guest Hub is what individual guests use on their phones.

Audience Display is designed for the venue television, projector or larger display.

It can show selected prompts, games, scores, photos and live moments to the entire room without exposing your private DJ controls.
